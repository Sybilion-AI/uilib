Tabs organise related content into panels that share the same space, with only one panel visible at a time. Three variants — button, link, and outline — cover different visual weights, each in small, medium, and large sizes.

## When to use

- To split a page or card into distinct views of related content, such as overview and details.
- When users switch between views often and switching should feel instant.
- For grouping settings or data categories that do not need to be visible at once.

## When not to use

- For sequential steps that must be completed in order; use a stepper or wizard instead.
- When users need to compare the contents of two panels side by side.
- For full-page navigation between routes; use PageTabs or links instead.

## Do

- Keep tab labels short and parallel in structure, ideally one or two words.

## Don't

- Don't use more tabs than fit comfortably in the available width.
